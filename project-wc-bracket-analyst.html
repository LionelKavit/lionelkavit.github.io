The problem

Everyone fills out a bracket. Almost nobody can defend one.

Every World Cup, the group chat fills with brackets picked by flag and gut feel. And winning a small pool isn't even about being the most accurate — if everyone picks the chalk, being right wins you nothing. I wanted the knowledgeable friend you'd text: real odds for every matchup, and a straight answer on where a bold pick actually buys you an edge.

How it works

Consistency you can compute.

Tell the Analyst your pool size and how bold you feel, and about a minute later you have a complete, odds-backed bracket — yours to overrule pick by pick, then lock and export into your pool. Underneath is one engine: each matchup's Elo gap — the same rating method FIFA uses for its own World Ranking — becomes a win probability, the whole tournament plays out 50,000 times, and every odd on the page comes from those runs, pinned by 287 automated tests. The AI never computes any of it — it reads the engine's numbers and explains them, and if it can't back an answer with those numbers, it says so rather than invent one. Your risk setting never bends the simulation; it only decides which underdogs are worth backing for your pool size.

Reflection
Behind the build

I built the Bracket Analyst because I've been in that group chat — everyone fills out a bracket, nobody knows half the teams, everyone wants to beat their friends. The insight that shaped it: winning a small pool is about being differentiated in the right way, while keeping every number honest enough to hold up when someone checks it. This World Cup I trusted it with my own bracket, and the calls landed in public — every semifinalist, both finalists, and a champion pick that missed by one extra-time goal. I set out to build the knowledgeable friend you'd text; ending up as the tool's first proven user is the ending I didn't script.
