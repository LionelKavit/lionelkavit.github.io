The bracket predictor

One chat. A complete, odds-backed bracket.

Tell the Analyst your pool size and how bold you feel, and about a minute later you're looking at a complete World Cup bracket — every pick backed by real odds, ready to defend. The hard part happens invisibly: 48 teams, 12 groups, and the puzzle of the 8 best third-placed teams, all resolved into a filled-in knockout draw before you touch a thing.

From there it's yours. Keep the autofill or overrule it — every matchup shows its real head-to-head odds, and bold underdogs are flagged as you go. Happy with it? Lock it and export the CSV straight into your pool. It's the same autofill that seeded my bracket, graded below.

Personal reflection
Behind the build

I built the Bracket Analyst because I've been in that group chat — everyone fills out a World Cup bracket, nobody actually knows half the teams, and everyone secretly wants to beat their friends. I wanted to put the knowledgeable friend they'd text into an app.

The insight that shaped it: winning a small pool isn't about being the most accurate, it's about being differentiated in the right way — while keeping every number honest enough to hold up when someone checks it. Getting both right is the part I'm proudest of.

This World Cup made it concrete. I ran my own bracket through the engine — the model's autofill plus my own reads — and the calls landed in public: every semifinalist, both semifinal results, the exact final. I set out to build the knowledgeable friend you'd text; ending up as the tool's first proven user is the ending I didn't script. The graded bracket is one scroll up, misses included.

Why you can trust it

Consistency you can compute.

So how does it actually decide a match? Take one matchup. The engine turns the two teams' Elo gap — the same rating method FIFA uses for its own World Ranking — into a win probability, and draws the result from it. No vibes, no reputations, no pundit narratives. Now play the whole tournament out that way, 50,000 times, and you get every standing, knockout odd, and pool verdict on this page — rules locked down by an automated test suite so the numbers don't drift. The AI never computes any of it; it only reads what the engine produced and explains it in plain English.

It's also why the model kept faith with Spain and Argentina while the form narrative crowned France and England. Form is the last three games; Elo is years of results. Consistency beat form in both semifinals — the graded bracket above did the arguing. And your settings never bend the simulation: pool size and risk appetite only decide which underdogs are worth backing — the odds themselves are untouchable.

Upset watch · pool-winning strategy

Winning a pool isn't the same as being right.

Winning a small pool is about being differentiated, not just accurate — if everyone picks the chalk, being right wins you nothing. That's the upset-watch engine's job: it doesn't just compute the odds of being right, it calculates the statistical payoff of a bold pick against your pool size and risk appetite. The "You vs. the Model" view shows how far your bracket strays from the favourites, so you stand out enough to win without blowing it up. That calibration is why my bracket's deep branches held even when the tournament's paths shifted — quarterfinal calls that outlived the opponents they were supposed to meet.

The group stage

The part no scoreboard shows you.

The dashboard tracks all 12 groups live — every team marked Through, In contention, in the 3rd-place race, or Out, with a Next-Round % beside it. The top two of each group always advance; the twist is that the 8 best third-placed teams out of the 12 groups go through as well, and working out which ones means weighing results across every group at once — the part no single group's table can show you. Tap any team for the exact scenarios that decide its fate. It's the same group engine that seeded the Round of 32 my bracket was graded on above.
